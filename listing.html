Golden Hour Study captures the precise quality of late afternoon light across the Castilian plateau — that luminous forty-minute window when the sun flattens and the landscape seems to hold its breath. The painting was made on location over three consecutive evenings in July 2025, near the village of Buitrago del Lozoya, north of Madrid.

"I wanted to paint not the landscape, but the quality of the air above it — the way heat lingers in the light even as the day cools."

The paint is applied in dense, confident impasto, the knife marks still visible beneath the final glazes. The palette moves from ochre and cadmium orange at the horizon through transparent rose madder into a cool violet sky. The work is painted on Belgian linen, double-primed with lead white.

This is an original one-of-a-kind work. It arrives rolled or stretched (as specified at checkout), accompanied by a hand-signed certificate of authenticity and a letter from the artist describing the circumstances of the painting's creation.

I painted this piece at the end of a long summer spent working outdoors in Castile. By July the light is extreme — brutal at noon, magnificent at dusk. The plateau strips everything back to essentials: earth, air, light.

I set up my easel three evenings in a row at the same spot on a low ridge, working quickly, trying to catch the same forty minutes of light each time. The first session was exploratory. The second, I overcorrected. The third, something gave — the paint and the light found each other, and I knew it was done.

This is the kind of work I make for myself. I'm glad it's going to a collector.

— Elena Vasquez, September 2025
